# Hold Notifications

<FeatureIntro>

Launch0 can hold notifications from the apps you choose and deliver them in one batch later, so a group chat or a shopping app can't break your focus every few minutes. You pick the apps and the hold time; Launch0 parks their notifications until the window ends, or until you release them yourself.

It works on your device with Android's own notification controls. Nothing is sent anywhere: Launch0 declares no internet permission.

</FeatureIntro>

## Set it up

1. Long-press the home screen to open **Settings**.
2. Under **Do Not Disturb**, turn on **Hold notifications** and grant Launch0 notification access when Android asks.
3. Set the **Hold duration**: 30, 45, 60, 90, 120 or 180 minutes.
4. Open **Apps to hold** and tap the apps whose notifications should wait.

## How the hold window works

The first notification from a chosen app starts a hold window of the length you picked. Every notification those apps post during the window is held, and when it ends, Android delivers them all together. The next notification after that starts a new window.

While notifications are held:

- **A count appears beside the app** on the home screen and in the app drawer. Tap it to release that app's notifications right away.
- **An "On hold" card** joins your home-screen widgets, listing each app with held notifications. Tap a row to release them.

Ongoing notifications — calls, music, downloads and other running services — are never held.

## FAQ

### Which Android versions does it work on?

Android 8.0 and later. Holding uses Android's notification snooze, which older versions don't have; on Android 7.x, notifications pass through untouched.

### Is this the same as Android's Do Not Disturb?

No. Android's Do Not Disturb mutes interruptions across your phone. Launch0 holds notifications from only the apps you pick, keeps them out of your notification shade, and brings them back together when the window ends. Everything else, including calls and alarms, works as normal.

### Does Launch0 read my notifications?

It needs notification access to see which app posted a notification so it can hold it, and to count what's waiting. It doesn't store notification contents or send them anywhere. Android keeps the held notifications and re-posts them when they're released. See the [privacy policy](/privacy#notification-access-do-not-disturb).
